Note. Pseudogene load, rate models. Under the same Bakta version and database, the 260 cultured isolates carry fewer pseudogenes than MAGs of their own clade (RGM p = 4 × 10⁻⁵, SGM p = 3 × 10⁻⁶), because reading frames truncated at contig boundaries are annotated as pseudogenes; binning therefore inflates pseudogene counts instead of masking them. FSM carries 1.17 pseudogenes per Mb, compared with 1.77 in RGM and 3.32 in SGM (FSM vs RGM, p = 7 × 10⁻⁶), and a negative binomial model with genome size as offset gives an FSM/RGM rate ratio of 0.69, or 0.60 with contigs per Mb as covariate (p = 2 × 10⁻⁹; Table S6). Because isolates and MAGs share the same annotation, the comparison was also run on all 503 ingroup genomes with retained per-genome counts (243 MAGs and 260 cultured isolates): the median pseudogene count was 4 in FSM and 9 in both RGM and SGM, with a rate ratio of 0.68, or 0.43 with contigs per Mb (p = 1 × 10⁻¹¹; Table S6). The MAG-only median of 18 in SGM reflects this binning inflation.

Note. Protein-length proxy for pseudogenisation. Protein length provides an independent indicator of pseudogenisation, since decayed reading frames yield truncated proteins. FSM proteins have a median length of 274 amino acids with 10.4% shorter than 100 residues, compared with 273 and 9.4% in RGM and 264 and 12.0% in SGM; in the leprosy clade the median length is 105 amino acids with 45.9% shorter than 100 (Table S6). No evidence of widespread truncation is therefore apparent in FSM.

Note. Restricting the comparison to the fragmentation range occupied by both clades leaves the rate ratio essentially unchanged, so the pseudogene deficit is not an artefact of FSM assemblies being more fragmented. Both models carry contigs per Mb as a covariate and log genome size as an offset.
