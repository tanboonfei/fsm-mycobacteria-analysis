S7. Phylogenetically corrected FSM vs RGM comparisons (one origin; raw tests shown for contrast)

Note. Phylogenetically corrected contrasts. Because FSM has a single origin, phylogenetically corrected tests are the primary comparisons (Table S7; methods). Relative to RGM, four contrasts remained significant under the phylogenetic model: genome size (β = −2.03 Mb, p = 0.025), proteome acidity (β = −0.31 percentage points, p = 0.013), opsin presence (phyloglm β = +2.5, p = 5 × 10⁻⁵) and urease loss (β = −4.2, p = 3 × 10⁻³). The 1.0-point coding-density difference was not significant (p = 0.15). Estimated λ was at or near 1 in every model, and additional genomes contribute little independent information and no single clade-membership test is decisive. Across the full ingroup, including SGM, the genome-size contrast also held (β = −1.96 Mb, p = 0.025; Table S8), although a Brownian null did not reject drift (p = 0.6), and uncorrected genome-by-genome tests were consistent in direction (Fig. S1). The closest large-genome sister clade of FSM is a 42-genome RGM group with a median genome size of 5.82 Mb; repeating the corrected tests against this clade alone strengthened the genome-size (β = −1.95 Mb, p = 0.006) and opsin (β = +3.11, p = 3 × 10⁻⁶) contrasts, whereas urease loss, ectoine loss and proteome acidity were no longer significant (p = 0.2, 1.0 and 0.08; Table S7).
