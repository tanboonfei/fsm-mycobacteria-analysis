Note. Gene-family recovery under fragmentation. At the clustering thresholds used, 8.5% of proteins in a fragmented, 95.6%-complete genome were no longer recovered, with slightly greater loss among the longest proteins (87% recovery in the top length decile compared with 93% in the shortest). For a family that is actually present to be scored as lost clade-wide, it would have to be missed in 54 of the 59 high-completeness FSM MAGs; under independent loss this has probability 5 × 10⁻⁵², and at a 40% miss rate the expected number of spurious clade-wide losses among 1,245 families is 2 × 10⁻¹³. All losses were additionally verified by homology search against the contigs.

Note. Gene-caller disagreement. Fragmentation reduced Bakta coding density by 3.00 percentage points but Prodigal coding density by only 0.04, reproducing the observed difference between the two callers (+2.69 simulated; +2.44 across MAGs; +0.46 across reference assemblies). Fragmentation added one Bakta pseudogene per genome, compared with observed isolate-to-MAG offsets of 3 in RGM and 11.5 in SGM; all of these inflate counts, and FSM nevertheless has the lowest pseudogene load in the ingroup (Table S4b).

Note. Completeness is this study's CheckM2 v1.0.1 estimate. The gap between the clades is essentially flat as the floor is raised and the two extrapolated intervals are far apart; incompleteness accounts for roughly 0.2 Mb of a 2.7 Mb difference. FSM has no cultured isolate and no closed assembly, and the comparison cannot be made on finished genomes within the clade; the isolate-against-MAG offsets measured in RGM and SGM (Table S4) and the in-silico fragmentation of closed genomes (Table S4b) bound the artefact from the other two directions. Marker-based completeness may itself be depressed in a genuinely reduced genome that has lost universal single-copy markers, in which case FSM completeness is underestimated and this correction is conservative.
