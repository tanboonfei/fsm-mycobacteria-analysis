Denominators used throughout S15 and S15e. Panel: the 1,245 gene families held at ≥80% prevalence in both RGM and SGM, and so present before FSM diverged. 53: those falling to ≤10% prevalence in the high-completeness FSM MAGs, i.e. lost clade-wide, a criterion based on prevalence in extant genomes. 33: the subset of the panel reconstructed as confidently present at the node preceding the FSM stem and confidently absent at the FSM crown, placing the loss on the stem branch; all 33 are among the 53. The 33 and the 53 are therefore nested, but they are defined by different criteria, reconstructed state and extant prevalence.

Tree: 178 high-completeness genomes (59 FSM, 83 RGM, 36 SGM); MAG absence is confounded with assembly gaps below 95% completeness.

Method: ML marginal ancestral states (ape::ace, ARD) for the probability the family is already absent at the FSM crown node,

Reading: the crown node is reconstructed as already lacking the family for 48 of 52, and for none is absence at that node unlikely.

Note. Ancestral reconstruction at two nodes. Under the equal-rates Mk model, 1,086 families (87%) are confidently present at the FSM crown node, 44 (3.5%) confidently absent and 115 (9.2%) unresolved, whereas at the node subtending the stem branch only 6 are confidently absent. Family by family, 33 are confidently present at the preceding node and confidently absent at the crown, placing their loss between the divergence of FSM and the diversification of the extant clade. A further 5 are absent at the crown from an unresolved ancestral state, none reverses, and no family present at the crown has subsequently been lost clade-wide (Table S15).

Note. Panel and model sensitivity of stem losses. The number of stem losses is more sensitive to the reconstruction model than to the panel definition or genome set. An all-rates-different model reclassifies a substantial minority of families (Methods). Repeating the classification on MMseqs2 clusters instead of named families gives 63 clade-wide losses among 1,179 conserved clusters, and applying the same clustering to all 822 ingroup genomes, which adds 258 cultured isolates and 321 reference MAGs, gives 99 of 1,292; the value reported here is therefore the most conservative of the three (Table S15). Because FSM contains no cultured isolate, an isolate-based comparison within the clade is not possible, but the direction of the result is consistent across all panels. The subsequent radiation is deep: mean crown-to-tip depth within FSM is 0.26 substitutions per site, compared with 0.38 across the whole ingroup; the stem losses predate most of the divergence now present within the clade. This analysis is restricted to the conserved-family panel and does not date the genome-wide difference in CDS count.

Note. CDS decomposition. Among annotated MAGs, FSM genomes contain a mean of 3,359 CDS compared with 5,787 in RGM, i.e. 2,428 fewer genes in genomes 2.41 Mb smaller, or approximately 990 bp per missing gene. This difference was decomposed by MMseqs2 cluster across the 96 FSM and 97 RGM annotated MAGs: clusters absent from FSM account for 2,919 CDS per genome, and gross positive differences in clusters retained by both clades for a further 829, offset by 1,319 CDS in clusters enriched in FSM (2,919 + 829 − 1,319), recovering the observed difference of 2,428 to within rounding (Table S15).

Note. Copy-number and prevalence components. The gross positive differences are dispersed across 3,895 clusters, with the 200 largest accounting for 23% of the total. Across all 7,870 clusters present in both clades, positive and negative differences together give a net difference of 227 CDS, of which 167 are attributable to the number of genomes carrying a cluster and 60 to the number of copies per carrier; the difference therefore reflects both prevalence and copy number. Losses are concentrated in lipid metabolism, transcription, energy production, secondary-metabolite and amino-acid metabolism and ion transport (Table S15). Paralogue families are uniformly reduced, with no collapse of a few large families of the kind seen for the PE/PPE expansions in the pathogens.

Note. Rates are pooled across all 1,245 genus-conserved families so that gain and loss are identifiable; per-family two-rate (ARD) fits are not, which is why 1,006 of 1,245 return exactly 0.500 in Table S15e. A pooled fit with both rates free rails at the gain bound (gain 50.0, loss 3.4), indicating that scattered single-genome absences are absorbed as fast switching rather than as phylogenetic signal, and the rate ratio was fixed at a series of values instead. "Expected stem losses" is the threshold-free sum over families of P(present at the preceding node) x P(absent at the crown) and is the quantity to compare across models: the confident counts move sharply because P(crown present) crosses the 0.05 line, not because the reconstruction itself changes. Stem-branch localisation is robust to moderate asymmetry, giving 37-58 expected losses for gain between 1x and one tenth of loss, and weakens only under a near-Dollo model (gain = loss/100, 12 expected). The main-text figure of 33 is the per-family equal-rates count at the 0.95/0.05 threshold.
