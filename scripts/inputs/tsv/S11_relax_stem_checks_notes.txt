Note. RELAX stem branch and branch definitions. Retained core genes are under strong purifying selection in both reduced lineages, and the contrast between them does not depend on the branch definition. Labelling all internal and terminal crown branches instead of the stem and tips gave K = 1.046 for FSM (LRT 13.7; p = 2 × 10⁻⁴) and 0.771 for the leprosy clade (LRT 282; p < 10⁻¹⁶) against the same reference (Table S11). When the FSM stem branch, which carries the reconstructed gene losses, was tested alone, K was 0.94 and the likelihood ratio did not reject K = 1 (LRT 2.1; p = 0.15; Table S11h); the null and alternative models differed by only 0.07 AICc units, which means that K is not identifiable on this branch. The branch-set dN/dS values from the same fits are more informative and point in the opposite direction: dN/dS on the stem alone is 0.363, compared with 0.107 for the reference, a 3.4-fold elevation, whereas the leprosy clade shows a 2.0-fold elevation; the FSM stem, however, is a single branch of summed length 0.065 compared with 0.333 for the leprosy set (Table S11i). When the stem is pooled with the 14 terminal branches, dN/dS falls to 0.091, indistinguishable from the reference. An elevated dN/dS on a single short branch is weak evidence, subject to upward bias, and is consistent with an episode of adaptive change as well as with relaxed selection; no branch-site model was fitted to distinguish these. It is nevertheless the only statistic in this study that suggests altered selection on the branch carrying the gene losses.

Note. Synonymous-site saturation. The randomisation ceiling for third codon positions at 84.8% GC is 0.625, well below the 0.75 expected under equal base frequencies, and divergence between each test set and the reference reaches 79% of this ceiling for FSM and 89% for the leprosy clade (Table S11). Divergence of this magnitude limits the resolution of branch-specific dS; these summaries do not determine the direction of any resulting bias in K.

Note. The two lineages were previously compared only through separate runs against a common unreduced reference, which gives two point estimates and no test of the difference between them. Fitting them against each other supplies that test. RELAX K is not symmetric under exchange of the branch sets: it measures how far the test distribution is drawn towards neutrality relative to the reference; the orientation with the more differentiated distribution as the reference rails at the bound. The interpretable orientation is the first row, in which K = 0.76 is close to the value the leprosy clade returns against the unreduced relatives (0.7579, Table S11), and the difference between the two reduced lineages is significant on its own terms.

Note. Third-position divergence reaches 79% of the randomisation ceiling for FSM-reference pairs and 89% for leprosy-reference pairs (Table S11c), and branch-specific synonymous rates are compromised and the direction of any resulting bias in K was not determined. Modelling synonymous rate variation addresses this directly. Both K values move slightly away from the values in Table S11 and both fit the data better, but neither conclusion changes: FSM shows no relaxation and the leprosy clade does. The site-class proportions change more than K, as expected when dS variation is absorbed by a separate process rather than by omega.
