Note. Genome-wide codon usage. Genome-wide codon-usage statistics computed from all predicted CDS instead of the core set, distinguished the clades in uncorrected comparisons but not after phylogenetic correction. Codon bias is weaker in FSM than in RGM and GC3 is lower; these two quantities are mechanically coupled, and the residual from the ENC plot (Nc against GC3), which measures bias beyond that attributable to base composition, runs in the opposite direction, leaving FSM with marginally more bias than its base composition predicts. Under a Pagel’s λ model, none of the three statistics separates the clades (Fig. 4b; tables S12b, S12c). Ribosomal-protein genes are less codon-optimised in FSM, the ribosomal-to-bulk difference is smaller, and within-population πN/πS tends to be higher, all on uncorrected comparisons only (Tables S12, S13; Fig. 4c). The ordering is consistent with the selection test: codon bias has collapsed in the host-restricted decayed genomes (Nc 51.3–51.6; Table S10), whereas FSM lies close to the unreduced pole, exceeding RGM by 1.8 codons of Nc on the per-genome values shown in Fig. 4b. Mycobacterium lepraemurium illustrates that genome decay and loss of codon bias need not coincide: it is the least reduced of the decayed genomes at 4.05 Mb, carries 1,139 annotated pseudogenes [70], and yet has the lowest Nc in the panel (33.9; Table S10). Its GC3 is also the highest in the panel, and under the coupling described above its Nc is close to that expected from base composition alone; no ENC-plot residual was computed for this genome, and its values were not recomputed under the uniform gene caller, and they are not compared with the clade medians (Table S10).
